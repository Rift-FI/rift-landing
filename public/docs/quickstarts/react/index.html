    React quickstart

    You will build a page where a customer signs in, secures their wallet with a passkey and sends 5 USDC on Base. The send starts on your server, which keeps the API key; the customer approves it with their passkey in the Rift widget.

    You need: React 18 or 19, a sandbox project and its API key from the Rift dashboard, and HTTPS in production (WebAuthn allows http://localhost in development).

    1. Register your origins. In the dashboard, open your project → Origins and add every origin that renders the widget, for example http://localhost:5173 and https://app.example.com. Passkey setup and approvals inside the widget are rejected from origins that are not listed.

    2. Install.

      Approvals need @rift-finance/react 0.4 or later and @rift-finance/wallet 4 or later. @rift-finance/react runs in the browser. @rift-finance/wallet runs on your server in step 5.

    3. Wrap your app. environment="sandbox" points the widget at https://widget.sandbox.riftfi.com, which talks to the sandbox API. Mount <RiftAuth /> once; it renders nothing until you open it.

      After sign-in, if the customer has no passkey yet, the modal shows Secure your wallet with a passkey. New customers get a new Safe; existing customers keep their address. user.passkeySetup.status tells you which happened.

    4. Sign in and send. useRift() gives you the session; getAccessToken() refreshes it when it is close to expiry. useRiftApproval() shows the approval widget for a 202 response and resolves with the outcome.

    5. Add the server route. Your server forwards the customer’s token to Rift with your API key. Without a passkey proof, Rift answers 202 APPROVAL_REQUIRED instead of moving funds.

    6. Try it. Sign in with an email OTP, create the passkey, press Send. The widget shows what is being approved and asks for the passkey. On approval, outcome.result.transactionHash is the on-chain transaction.

    What just happened

    Section titled “What just happened”
    • Sign-in (email, phone, Google or Apple) created a session. It did not create a key on Rift’s side.
    • The passkey you created is the only owner of the customer’s Safe.
    • Your server asked Rift to send funds and got an approval request, because it cannot sign.
    • The customer’s passkey signed the exact operation inside the widget; the Safe verified it on-chain.
